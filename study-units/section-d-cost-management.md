# Section D: Cost Management

Cost management covers tracking, measuring, and analyzing the resource consumption of an organization.

## Cost Objects & Measurement Contexts
A **cost object** is any item, target, or activity for which a specific cost measurement is desired (e.g., products, processes, departments).
*   **Intermediate Cost Objects:** Accumulate costs temporarily (e.g., Work-in-Process inventory).
*   **Final Cost Objects:** The ultimate accumulation target (e.g., Finished Goods inventory).

## Traceability & The Object-Dependent Rule
Traceability is not inherent to a cost; it depends entirely on the chosen object of measurement:
*   **Direct Costs:** Easily and economically traceable to a cost object.
*   **Indirect Costs:** Cannot be easily traced; must be pooled and allocated using a **cost driver** (a causal factor).
*   **The Object-Dependent Rule:** A supervisor's salary is a *direct cost* of the Production Department, but an *indirect cost* of an individual unit of product.

## Cost vs. Expense Lifecycle
*   **Unexpired Costs:** Capitalized on the Balance Sheet as **Assets** (e.g., raw materials, prepaid insurance).
*   **Expired Costs:** Recognized on the Income Statement as **Expenses** (matching revenue) or **Losses** (no future benefit).
*   **Spoilage:**
    *   *Normal Spoilage:* Inherent in efficient operations; capitalized as a product cost.
    *   *Abnormal Spoilage:* Avoidable inefficiency; expensed immediately as a period loss.

## Costs of Quality (COQ)
1.  **Prevention Costs:** Preventing defects before they happen (training, quality circles).
2.  **Appraisal Costs:** Inspecting and testing products (laboratory tests, inspection equipment).
3.  **Internal Failure Costs:** Scrap, rework, and re-testing before shipment.
4.  **External Failure Costs:** Warranty repairs, returns, recalls, and lost customer goodwill.
